# HELIOS — Crew Health Monitor

A prototype of on-board health monitoring software for long-duration missions. It collects
physiological, behavioral and environmental indicators, compares each one with the astronaut's
**own preflight baseline**, and turns any deviation into an explained finding with concrete steps.
Astronauts can then act on their health without waiting for a 14-minute-each-way ground loop.

> Prototype: all sensor data is simulated. This is not a medical device.

## Run it

No build step and no dependencies. It runs fully offline, as it would on board.

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8173
```

Then open http://localhost:8173. Opening `index.html` directly also works.

## What it does

Run `npm start` (or the command above) and open http://localhost:8173.

| Screen | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **My health** | Readiness score, six health-domain cards, ranked findings with "do now" steps, today's tasks, cabin environment, ground-link latency |
| **Trends** | Per-indicator charts against your personal normal range, limits and mission events; deviation, trend per week, 14-day projection, data coverage |
| **Daily check-in** | Two-minute self-report: sleep, mood, energy, stress, symptoms, manual vitals, privacy choice. Results are re-evaluated immediately |
| **Alertness test** | PVT-B psychomotor vigilance test (1 or 3 min), scored for mean RT, lapses (>355 ms) and false starts |
| **Protocols** | Ten on-board procedures (SPE shelter, sleep recovery, CO₂ headache, bone countermeasures, immune, SANS…) with step tracking and escalation criteria |
| **Downlink report** | Compact summary for the flight surgeon: queue for the next window, export JSON, or print |
| **Crew (CMO)** | Crew Medical Officer overview. Private behavioral entries stay hidden |

| **Model validation** | Results on real Inspiration4 crew blood data (see below) |

Across the app:

- **Cinematic interface:** animated deep-space starfield, a start-up sequence, glass panels and staggered entrance animations. Motion is turned off for anyone who prefers reduced motion.
- **Mission trajectory:** the Earth-to-Mars transfer arc with the vehicle's position, days to arrival and signal delay.
- **Body status map:** a body silhouette with pulsing hotspots per health domain; click one to open its trends.
- **Voice briefing:** a spoken summary of readiness, top findings and due tasks (Web Speech API, works offline).
- **Command palette:** press ⌘K, Ctrl+K or `/` to jump to any view, indicator, protocol or crew member, or run an action.
- **Sleep-period display** (moon icon): a red-shifted, low-blue-light theme.

## Feature modules

New capabilities live in `js/features/` and plug into the app via a small API (`H.app`) and event bus (`H.bus`), so `app.js` stays the shell.

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Lab panels** (`labs.js`) | Enter blood count + metabolic panels; the 8 clinical-panel models validated on Inspiration4 data (`js/lab-model.js`, generated by `tools/evaluate.js`) score the latest draw against your own preflight draws. "Load real example" loads an actual Inspiration4 crew member's draws. |
| **Import wearables** (`wearables.js`) | Apple Health `export.xml` (streamed, works with large exports) and any CSV from Fitbit, Garmin, Oura, Whoop or a spreadsheet — columns and units are auto-matched, then confirmed. |
| **What works for you** (`outcomes.js`) | Logging a protocol snapshots its target indicators; 2–7 days later HELIOS checks whether they improved and keeps a per-person track record. |
| **Symptom triage** (`triage.js`) | Offline yes/no guides — chest pain, vision change, head injury, burns, allergic reaction — ending in steps and the kit items needed. |
| **Medical kit** (`medkit.js`) | Shared inventory with expiry in mission days, radiation-sensitive and prescription flags, and a per-person medication log. |
| **Day planner** (`planner.js`) | 24-hour circadian plan (light, caffeine, peak work, nap, exercise, wind-down, sleep), adjusted to your sleep debt and alertness results. |
| **Cognition tests** (`cognition.js`) | Digit span (working memory) and math processing (speed), compared with your own history, alongside the PVT. |
| **Nutrition & hydration** (`nutrition.js` + engine) | New health domain: body mass, fluid and energy intake, with its own rule and protocol P-NUT-01. |
| **Space weather** (`spaceweather.js`) | Solar-particle-event warning with countdown, storm-shelter timer and dose received / avoided. |
| **Settings** (`settings.js`) | Language (English, Français, Deutsch, 日本語, Русский, हिन्दी — navigation, headings and common controls), glove mode, voice control, install, and encrypted sync. |

**Offline install.** `manifest.webmanifest` + `sw.js` make HELIOS installable and usable with no connection (served over http/https).

**Encrypted sync.** `node tools/sync-server.js` runs a relay that only stores ciphertext. Devices encrypt with AES-256-GCM using a key derived (PBKDF2, 210k iterations) from the crew passphrase; the relay can hold bundles back to simulate the Earth light-time delay.

**Voice control** uses the browser's speech recognition, which Chrome processes online — it is the one feature that needs a connection.

## Personalization: HELIOS adapts to you

Open **My profile** (or click **Make it mine** on the dashboard) to create your own profile. HELIOS
then judges every reading against *your* normal instead of a population average. The demo crew stay
available in the crew menu.

- **Your data in.** Log readings in the daily check-in (sleep, resting HR, HRV, blood pressure,
  temperature, exercise, mood, stress, symptoms) and take the alertness test. You can also import a
  CSV with a `date` column plus any indicator IDs (`rhr`, `hrv`, `sbp`, `sleep`, `pvt`, …). The
  profile page has a template.
- **Preflight baseline.** CSV rows dated before your mission start, or values typed in during
  setup, become your preflight baseline.
- **Learns, then adapts.** Without preflight data, HELIOS calibrates from your first 7 readings.
  After that the baseline adapts slowly: a 30-day half-life over readings more than 14 days old,
  skipping outliers. The 14-day lag means a developing problem doesn't quietly become your new normal.
- **Realistic spread.** Your spread is blended with the population reference (4 pseudo-readings),
  so a handful of readings can't produce an unrealistically tight band.
- **"This is normal for me."** Re-baselines one indicator on your last 14 days (logged, and
  reversible).
- **Alert sensitivity.** Choose Sensitive (1.5 / 2.5 SD), Standard (2 / 3) or Conservative (2.5 / 3.5).
- **Private by design.** Everything stays in this browser. You can export a backup, restore it, or
  delete your profile and data.

## Health domains and indicators

- **Cardiovascular**: resting HR, HRV (RMSSD), systolic BP
- **Bone & Muscle**: estimated hip BMD change (vs. the expected in-flight curve), urinary calcium, countermeasure exercise, grip strength
- **Immune**: lymphocytes, CRP, salivary EBV/VZV shedding, core temperature
- **Radiation**: daily dose rate, career dose with a projection to mission end against the 600 mSv limit
- **Sleep & Behavioral**: sleep duration and efficiency, mood, stress, PVT reaction time
- **Neuro-ocular (SANS)**: retinal nerve-fiber-layer change (OCT), headache days
- **Habitat**: ppCO₂, temperature, humidity, sleep-station noise (shared exposures that often explain symptoms)

## How assessment works (`js/engine.js`)

1. **Personal baseline.** Deviation in SD from the astronaut's preflight mean (or from an expected in-flight trajectory for bone loss). 2 SD → *Monitor*, 3 SD → *Action*.
2. **Absolute limits.** Medical thresholds per indicator (*Monitor / Action / Urgent*).
3. **Trend.** Linear trend over 21+ days, projected 14 days ahead, so slow drifts are caught before they cross a limit.
4. **Rules.** These combine indicators and symptoms into findings. For example, sleep loss together with slower PVT gives "Sleep loss is slowing your reactions", and headaches together with high cabin CO₂ point to the scrubber fault. Each finding lists its evidence, the steps to take, a linked protocol and a recommended route: self-manage, CMO, flight surgeon, or immediate crew response.
5. **Readiness** is 100 minus capped penalties for each domain.

## Validation on real astronaut data

`tools/evaluate.js` tests the detection methods on open clinical data from the four
**SpaceX Inspiration4** crew, taken from NASA's Open Science Data Repository:

- complete blood count (OSD-569)
- metabolic panel, serum cardiovascular panel and serum immune panel (OSD-575)

Each crew member has 7 blood draws: 3 preflight and 4 post-flight. The raw CSVs are in `data/osdr/`.

**Task:** using each person's own preflight draws as the baseline, single out the draw taken the
day after landing (R+1) from normal draws. Normal draws are the recovery draws, plus each preflight
draw scored against the other two. That gives 4 positives and 24 negatives.

**Method:** leave-one-crew-out. The held-out person is never used to fit SDs, select features,
train weights or set thresholds.

```bash
node tools/evaluate.js   # writes data/validation.json and js/validation-data.js
```

| Run 3 (blood count + metabolic panel, corrected) | AUROC | AUROC (post-flight only) | R+1 ranked first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical reference ranges | 0.44 | 0.45 | 1 / 4 |
| HELIOS rules (personal baseline, ≥3 SD) | 0.34 | 0.38 | 0 / 4 |
| Unsupervised anomaly score | 0.35 | 0.40 | 0 / 4 |
| **Spaceflight-signature model** | **0.79** (p = 0.037) | **0.92** | **3 / 4** |
| Logistic regression (L2) | 0.73 | 0.88 | 3 / 4 |

**Final models.** Run 3 above was hand-picked, so two follow-ups test that choice more fairly:

| Final model | AUROC | AUROC (post-flight only) | R+1 ranked first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ensemble:** rank-average of all 24 configurations (3 panel sets × 2 models × 4 feature counts) | **0.85** (p = 0.012) | **0.96** | **3 / 4** |
| **Nested search:** best of the 24 chosen by inner leave-one-crew-out on the training crew only | 0.68 (p = 0.15) | 0.65 | 1 / 4 |

The nested search is the most honest estimate of picking one "best" model. It scores lower than the
hand-picked run 3 because, with 4 people, which configuration wins is mostly noise. Averaging the
configurations avoids that, but the ensemble was designed after runs 1–3, so treat it as promising
rather than proven.

Run 1 (all 113 lab values, chosen before seeing any results) peaked at an AUROC of 0.64. Runs 2–3
were added after looking at run 1, so their scores are optimistic. With 4 people, no result is
significant once corrected for multiple comparisons. At the threshold learned on the other crew,
sensitivity is 0–25%, so the models rank well but are not calibrated for alarms. The app's
**Model validation** page shows every run.

## Structure

```
index.html
css/styles.css
js/data.js        mission, crew, indicator catalogue, deterministic simulator
js/engine.js      baseline / limit / trend assessment and finding rules
js/protocols.js   on-board procedures
js/charts.js      dependency-free SVG sparkline and trend charts
js/app.js         views, check-in, PVT, report, interactions
```

Check-ins, test results, protocol progress and the downlink queue are saved in the browser's `localStorage`.

## Next steps toward a flight system

- Replace the simulator with real device adapters (Bio-harness, actigraphy, dosimeter, analyzers)
- Use Bayesian baselines that update in flight, plus multivariate anomaly detection
- Encrypted on-board store, consent rules for each data type, and DTN bundles for the downlink
- Review of the rules and protocols by flight surgeons, and validation against analog-mission data
